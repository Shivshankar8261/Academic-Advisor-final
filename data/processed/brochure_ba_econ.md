## B.A. (Hons.) Economics: ROLE OF FISCAL POLICY IN THE INDIAN ECONOMY

### 1. Fiscal Policy
- Issue-wide strategy for taxation, spending, and borrowing to influence the economy.
- Objectives:
  - Stabilize inflation
  - Promote employment
  - Support economic growth
- "For-India-Target: It India's primary fiscal policy instrument.
- Revenue/Levy: Tax-based spending, or tax-cuts + investment and demand. Contractionary: Proposed spending or higher taxes + Controls inflation.

### 2. Economic Outcomes
- AD (nominal GDP) (Y) (Y= C+I+G+(X-M) indicated by the equation: AD = C+I+G+(X-M)
- Increased spending raises AD and GDP, but may also cause inflation.
- Inflation can result from a decreased spending. But taxation helps curb accelerated-driven price rises.
- Employment policies like MGNREGA show how fiscal intervention reduces rural unemployment.
- Fiscal policy also impacts macro and housing markets, influencing investor confidence.

### 3. Keynesian vs. Classical Perspectives
- Classical economists believe in minimal government intervention, arguing that free markets self-correct, distribute balanced budgets and private sector-led growth.
- Keynesian economics advocate for active fiscal intervention during downturns, using government spending to boost demand and reduce unemployment.
- India's approach blends both theories ->
- Classical policies: 1991, LPG reforms, FDI, privatization.
- Keynesian policies: 2008 crisis stimulus, COVID-19 relief measures.
- Multi-modal shifts (in-between): the two extremes during crises and fiscal restraint during stability.

### 4. Statistical Analysis of Indicator Trends
- Government Spending Increased post-COVID; for welfare and infrastructure but slower in 2024-25 due to fiscal consolidation.
- GDP Growth: Strong recovery after COVID-19, driven by investment and demand.
- Tax Revenues: Fell during the pandemic. But recovered due to increased collection and rising consumption.
- Inflation: High post-COVID rose with government stimulus but moderated with tax adjustments.
- Unemployment: Dropped during lockdowns. Improved with fiscal weapons, but rose again in 2023-24 due to global and technological shifts.

### 5. COVID-19 Fiscal Policy
- India's fiscal reaction gradually evolved, from a 1% direct resistance (cash transfers, food subsidies) and progressive to investment-led recovery (PLI schemes, capital expenditure).
- The ₹20 lakh crore Atmanirbhar Bharat package offered liquidity and credit guarantees, but was criticized for lacking direct fiscal stimulus.
- The fiscal deficit peaked at 9.2% of GDP in 2021, but recovery was aided by higher tax receipts and industrial expansion.
- Debate Concerns about the rising public debt, the policy stretch in consensus between short-term market and long-term economic stability.

## B.A. (Hons.) Economics: Message from the Dean

Liberal arts offer exciting frameworks for understanding the social, political, cultural, and economic institutions that shape our world. Drawing on perspectives from fields such as history, sociology, economics, philosophy, literature, and the arts, this approach encourages students to explore diverse ways of knowing. A liberal arts education fosters critical thinking, empathy, and a deeper appreciation of the complexity of society, preparing students to become thoughtful citizens, leaders and social observers.

The BA in Economics at the School of Liberal Arts and Sciences (SLAS), Vidyashilp University, is among the leading undergraduate programs in the country. Guided by highly qualified faculty, students build a strong foundation in the field of economics while gaining practical experience through internships.

As part of Vidyashilp University's vision of reimagining higher education, all undergraduate students take courses in the humanities and social sciences. This approach cultivates an interdisciplinary perspective while strengthening critical thinking, writing skills, and enhancing awareness about the complexities of Indian society and contemporary ecological challenges.
